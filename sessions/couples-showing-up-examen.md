# Showing Up Together: An Ignatian Evening for Couples
## A 90-Minute CLC Session on Emotional Agility and the Couples Examen

---

## Session Overview

**Theme**: Learning to "show up" to our emotions and each other through the lens of Ignatian spirituality and Susan David's Emotional Agility

**Outcomes**:
1. A new shared practice (Couples Examen) to use at home
2. Deeper understanding of each other's emotional inner world

**Format**: Zoom with private couple breakout rooms

## Detailed Session Guide

---

### OPENING & GROUNDING PRAYER (12 minutes)

#### Welcome (2 minutes)

> *"Welcome, everyone. Tonight we're going to explore what it means to 'show up' — to ourselves, to God, and to each other in our marriages. We'll draw on both the wisdom of Ignatian spirituality that grounds our CLC community and insights from Susan David's work on emotional agility.*
>
> *Before we begin, I invite you to find a comfortable position. If you're sitting near your spouse, you might gently place a hand on their arm or hold hands — whatever feels natural."*

#### Grounding Prayer: Settling Body, Heart, and Mind (10 minutes)

**🎵 MUSIC CUE**: Begin gentle instrumental music

> *"Let's begin by arriving — really arriving — into this space and this time together."*

**Body (3 minutes)**:
> *"Start by noticing your body. Feel your feet on the floor... the weight of your body in your chair... Let your shoulders drop. Unclench your jaw. Take a slow breath in through your nose... and release it through your mouth.*
>
> *Notice any tension you're carrying. Perhaps from the day. Perhaps from the week. You don't need to fix it — just notice. Breathe into those places.*
>
> *Your body has been faithful to you today. It carried you through meetings, conversations, tasks. Take a moment to simply thank your body for bringing you here."*

**Heart (3 minutes)**:
> *"Now, gently turn your attention to your heart. What emotions have visited you today? Perhaps there's been joy... frustration... worry... tenderness... fatigue...*
>
> *Don't judge what you find. Simply notice, as you would notice the weather. 'Ah, there's some anxiety.' 'There's gratitude.' Let each feeling be acknowledged.*
>
> *God sees your heart right now — sees everything in it — and looks on you with love."*

**Mind (3 minutes)**:
> *"Finally, notice your mind. What thoughts have been circling? What mental to-do lists are still running? What conversations are you replaying?*
>
> *Imagine placing each thought gently on a leaf floating down a stream. You don't need to solve anything right now. You don't need to figure anything out. For these 90 minutes, you are simply here.*
>
> *In the spirit of St. Ignatius, let us ask for the grace we need tonight..."*

**Closing the prayer**:
> *"Loving God, we thank you for the gift of marriage — for the one sitting beside us or near us. We ask for the grace to show up fully tonight: to ourselves, to you, and to each other. Open our hearts to see what you want us to see. Give us courage to be honest and tenderness to receive one another. We ask this through Christ our Lord. Amen."*

**🎵 MUSIC CUE**: Fade music

---

### TEACHING: "SHOWING UP" & THE IGNATIAN CONNECTION (15 minutes)

#### Introducing Emotional Agility (7 minutes)

> *"Susan David is a psychologist at Harvard who has spent her career studying how people navigate their inner world — particularly their emotions. She developed a framework called 'Emotional Agility,' and tonight we're focusing on its foundation: what she calls 'showing up.'*
>
> *So what does 'showing up' mean?"*

**Write in chat or share screen with these points:**

> *"'Showing up' means facing into our emotions with curiosity and compassion rather than avoiding them or being overwhelmed by them.*
>
> *Susan David identifies two common patterns most of us fall into:"*

**Pattern 1 — Bottling:**
> *"This is when we push emotions aside. We tell ourselves: 'I shouldn't feel this way.' 'It's not a big deal.' 'I need to stay strong.' We suppress, minimize, or ignore what we're feeling.*
>
> *In marriage, bottling might sound like: 'I'm fine' when you're not. Avoiding a conversation because it might get emotional. Keeping the peace by keeping quiet."*

**Pattern 2 — Brooding:**
> *"This is the opposite — when we get so caught up in our emotions that we lose perspective. We ruminate. We replay. We stew. The emotion takes over.*
>
> *In marriage, brooding might look like: Replaying an argument over and over. Catastrophizing: 'They always do this.' Getting stuck in resentment or worry."*

> *"Neither pattern is bad — they're human. But neither serves us or our marriages well.*
>
> *'Showing up' is the alternative. It means turning toward your emotions with curiosity: 'What am I actually feeling right now? What is this feeling trying to tell me?' It means holding your emotions with compassion — not drowning in them, not dismissing them."*

#### The Ignatian Connection (8 minutes)

> *"Here's what's beautiful for us as a CLC community: Ignatius figured this out 500 years ago.*
>
> *The Examen — this prayer practice at the heart of our spirituality — is fundamentally a practice of 'showing up.' Every day, we're invited to:*
> - *Notice what we're feeling*
> - *Name the movements of consolation and desolation*
> - *Approach our inner life with honesty and without judgment*
> - *Bring it all before God*
>
> *Ignatius didn't say, 'Push down your feelings and just be holy.' He didn't say, 'Wallow in your desolation.' He said: Pay attention. Notice. Be curious about what's happening in your soul. Bring it to God.*
>
> *This is 'showing up' — Ignatian style."*

**Bridge to marriage:**
> *"Tonight, we're going to apply this to our marriages. Because 'showing up' to yourself is one thing. But 'showing up' to another person — especially the person you share life with most intimately — requires even more courage.*
>
> *When was the last time you really showed up to what you were feeling in your marriage — not bottling, not brooding, but honestly acknowledging: 'This is what's happening in me'?*
>
> *And when was the last time you created space for your spouse to show up to you?"*

**Transition:**
> *"In a moment, I'm going to send you into breakout rooms — just you and your spouse. You'll have about 18 minutes for your first conversation. Before I send you, let me give you the questions."*

---

### FIRST COUPLE BREAKOUT: Understanding How We Show Up (18 minutes)

#### Setup (before sending to breakouts)

> *"You'll have 18 minutes together. Here are your questions — take them one at a time, and give each person space to share. Listen with curiosity, not problem-solving.*
>
> *I'll put these in the chat so you have them."*

**Questions for Couple Breakout #1:**

**Post in chat:**
```
BREAKOUT #1 QUESTIONS (18 minutes)

1. When it comes to difficult emotions, do you tend to be more of a "bottler" (pushing aside, minimizing) or a "brooder" (getting stuck, replaying)? Share an example if you're comfortable.

2. What makes it hard for you to "show up" to your own emotions? What gets in the way?

3. What helps you feel safe enough to share what's really going on inside you — with yourself, with God, with your spouse?

Take turns. Listen with curiosity, not advice.
```

> *"When you hear the chime, that means you have about 2 minutes left. I'll bring everyone back automatically."*

**🎵 MUSIC CUE**: Optional — have music playing as they enter breakouts

**FACILITATOR NOTE**: Set Zoom timer for 18 minutes with 2-minute warning.

---

### RETURN & INTRODUCING THE COUPLES EXAMEN (10 minutes)

#### Re-gathering (2 minutes)

> *"Welcome back. Take a breath. You've just done something countercultural — slowing down and actually talking about your inner world with each other.*
>
> *You don't need to share what you discussed. But I'm curious: How was that? I'll take one or two words in the chat. What's one word for how that conversation felt?"*

**Wait for a few chat responses. Acknowledge them briefly.**

#### Teaching: The Couples Examen (8 minutes)

> *"One of the outcomes I hoped for tonight is that you'd walk away with a practice you can do together at home. Something simple. Something sustainable. Something rooted in our Ignatian tradition.*
>
> *So I want to introduce you to the **Couples Examen** — an adaptation of the Daily Examen designed for spouses to pray together."*

**The Couples Examen — Five Movements:**

> *"This can be done in as little as 10-15 minutes. You might do it weekly, or even daily if you want to go deeper. Here's how it works:"*

**Share screen or post in chat:**

```
THE COUPLES EXAMEN — Five Movements

1. SETTLE TOGETHER
   Light a candle if you'd like. Take three slow breaths together.
   Acknowledge God's presence with you as a couple.

2. GRATITUDE
   Each person shares: "One thing I'm grateful for today..."
   (This can be anything — small or large, about your spouse or not.)

3. REVIEW THE DAY WITH HONESTY
   Each person shares: "Here's how I really am today..."
   Name the emotions present — not to fix them, just to be known.
   The listener's only job: receive, not repair.

4. NOTICE THE MOVEMENTS
   Each person reflects:
   - "A moment I felt life/energy today was..." (consolation)
   - "A moment I felt drained/heavy today was..." (desolation)
   Share if comfortable.

5. LOOK TOWARD TOMORROW
   Each person shares: "One thing I could use grace for tomorrow..."
   You might gently place a hand on your spouse as they share —
   a quiet intercession.

Close with a simple prayer: "Lord, bless our marriage. Amen."
```

> *"Notice what's happening here: each movement is an invitation to 'show up.' You're not fixing each other. You're not giving advice. You're witnessing each other — with curiosity and compassion — and inviting God into your marriage.*
>
> *In a moment, you're going to actually practice this together. You'll have about 22 minutes — enough time to go through all five movements without rushing."*

---

### SECOND COUPLE BREAKOUT: Practicing the Couples Examen (22 minutes)

#### Setup

> *"This is the heart of our time together. I'm sending you back into your breakout room to actually pray the Couples Examen together.*
>
> *Don't worry about doing it perfectly. Just move through the five steps. Be honest. Be gentle with each other.*
>
> *I'll put the steps in the chat again for reference."*

**Post in chat:**
```
BREAKOUT #2: PRAY THE COUPLES EXAMEN TOGETHER (22 minutes)

1. SETTLE — Breathe together. Acknowledge God's presence.

2. GRATITUDE — Each share: "One thing I'm grateful for today..."

3. REVIEW WITH HONESTY — Each share: "Here's how I really am today..."
   (Name emotions. Listener receives, doesn't fix.)

4. NOTICE MOVEMENTS — Each reflect:
   - "A moment of life/energy today..." (consolation)
   - "A moment of drain/heaviness today..." (desolation)

5. LOOK FORWARD — Each share: "One thing I could use grace for tomorrow..."
   (You might place a hand on your spouse as they share.)

Close: "Lord, bless our marriage. Amen."

Take your time. No need to rush.
```

**🎵 MUSIC CUE**: Gentle instrumental in breakouts (if possible) or as they transition

**FACILITATOR NOTE**: Set Zoom timer for 22 minutes with 2-minute warning.

---

### RETURN & WHOLE GROUP SHARING (5 minutes)

#### Re-gathering

> *"Welcome back. Thank you for giving this gift of presence to each other.*
>
> *We have just a few minutes, and I'd love to hear briefly from any couple who'd like to share. Just a sentence or two:*
>
> ***What was it like to pray the Couples Examen together? What did you notice?***
>
> *No pressure — only share if you'd like."*

**Allow 3-4 couples to share briefly (about 1 minute each).**

**If needed, prompt with:**
- "What surprised you?"
- "What felt life-giving about this?"
- "What might you carry forward?"

---

### CLOSING PRAYER (8 minutes)

#### Transition

> *"Let's close our time together in prayer. I invite you to once again get comfortable. If you'd like, you can hold your spouse's hand."*

**🎵 MUSIC CUE**: Begin gentle instrumental music (softer than before)

#### Gratitude (3 minutes)

> *"We begin where the Examen always begins — with gratitude.*
>
> *In the silence of your heart, call to mind one thing from tonight you're grateful for. It might be something your spouse shared... a moment of connection... a new insight... simply the gift of this time together.*
>
> *Hold that gratitude gently before God."*

**(30 seconds of silence)**

> *"If you'd like, you can unmute and speak your gratitude aloud in one word or phrase. Or you can hold it silently. Let's offer our gratitude together..."*

**(Allow those who want to share to speak briefly — just words/phrases, not sentences)**

> *"Thank you, Lord, for these gifts."*

#### Offering Intentions (4 minutes)

> *"Now, I invite you to bring before God any intentions on your heart — for your marriage, for your family, for something you're carrying.*
>
> *In the silence, lift these to God."*

**(30 seconds of silence)**

> *"Again, if you'd like, you can speak an intention aloud — just a word or phrase. We hold these together as a community, trusting them to God's care."*

**(Allow those who want to share to speak briefly)**

> *"Lord, we entrust these intentions to you."*

#### Blessing (1 minute)

> *"Let us pray:*
>
> *God of love, you brought each of these couples together. You have been present in their joys and their struggles, in the moments of deep connection and the moments of distance.*
>
> *Bless them now as they leave this time together. Give them courage to keep showing up — to themselves, to each other, and to you. When they are tempted to bottle, give them voice. When they are tempted to brood, give them perspective. When they need grace, pour it out in abundance.*
>
> *May the Couples Examen become a holy rhythm in their homes — a way of inviting you into the center of their marriages, again and again.*
>
> *We ask this in Jesus' name. Amen."*

**🎵 MUSIC CUE**: Let music play softly for a moment, then fade

#### Send-off

> *"Thank you for being here tonight. Thank you for taking this time for your marriage. May you continue to show up — with curiosity, with compassion, with courage — to one another.*
>
> *Go in peace."*

## Facilitator Notes

### Zoom Setup
- Create breakout rooms set to manual assignment (1 couple per room)
- Enable "Allow participants to return to main session at any time"
- Set timers: 18 min for Breakout #1, 22 min for Breakout #2
- Have questions/Examen steps ready to paste into chat

### Pacing Tips
- The opening grounding prayer is key — don't rush it
- Teaching sections can be slightly shortened if running long
- Protect the second breakout time (Examen practice) — this is the heart
- Closing prayer can flex slightly shorter if needed

### If Technology Fails
- If breakouts fail, couples can mute and turn cameras off for private time
- Have questions in a document ready to share screen
- Keep phone available to call Zoom support if needed

### Possible Adaptations
- **For longer sessions**: Add a third breakout for deeper sharing
- **For in-person**: Use separate rooms/corners for couple time
- **For retreat setting**: Add journaling time before breakouts
